# toponarch
**Pronunciation:** /təˈpɒnɑːrk/
**Etymology:** Greek tópos (place, configured locus / template) + árkhos (steward, administrator)
**Related SORM Concepts:** Ektoplax, Council of Elders, Configuration Space
**Relevant Musings:** Musing 09
**Status:** Proposed Neologism

## Core Definition (SORM Canonical Use)
A toponarch is the external steward entity or collective that administers a fixed configuration-space template (an ektoplax). It is not a creator, judge, soul, or soul-cluster. Its core functions are structural maintenance of the ektoplax, enforcement of the template’s boundary rules, and negotiation for alignment with a soul’s Council prior to incarnation. There exists one toponarch per ektoplax; there is no single universal meta-toponarch governing all templates.

## Distinction Guardrails
- Not the Council of Elders: The Council belongs to the soul’s collective.
- Not the Domain: The Domain is a separate, larger-scale collective of consciousnesses.
- Not a deity or creative source: It is administrative, not generative.
- Not part of any incarnating consciousness or its garbons.

## Literary & Technical Usage Examples
1. The toponarch upholds the invariant constraints of the ektoplax while coordinating pre-incarnation alignment with the soul’s Council.
2. No soul can override the foundational rules set by the toponarch, for the ektoplax persists independent of soul coherence.

## Rationale for This Term
This word replaces the longer descriptive phrase “Template Authority”. It isolates the administrative stewardship role, avoids religious baggage of words like “lord” or “ruler”, and clearly signals that its jurisdiction is bounded to one specific ektoplax.
