# Ectophore
**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛktəfɔːr/
**Etymology:** Greek *ektós* (outside, outward) + *phoros* (bearing, carrying)

## Definition
A material object that has undergone localized decoherence and slipped out of the observer’s primary frozen moment into an adjacent frequency basin. The object is not “lost”; it is ectophoric.

## What it replaces
"Vanished object", "poltergeist object", "thing that disappeared and reappeared", "spontaneous object translocation".

## Boundary Conditions
Only applies to material objects crossing vector boundaries through frequency instability. It does **not** describe mental imagery, apparitions, or purely perceptual shifts.

## ΨORM Cross-References
Localized frequency shift, decoherence, frozen moment

## Usage Example (Section XVII)
> The ring was an ectophore; it decohered from this frozen moment and persisted in a neighboring basin until frequency alignment permitted its return.
