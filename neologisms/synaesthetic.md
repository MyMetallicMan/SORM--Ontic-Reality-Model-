# Synaesthetic
**Pronunciation:** /ˌsɪnəsˈθetɪk/

## Etymology & Composition
Greek *syn-* (together, jointly) + *aisthēsis* (perception, sensory reception).
Standard usage refers to the well-known neurological phenomenon where stimulation of one sensory channel automatically triggers experience in another (e.g., seeing sound, tasting shapes). In SORM, the term is retained but extended far beyond conventional cross-sensory blending.

## SORM Role: Replaces / Supplements
Supplements the common definition of synaesthesia, and recontextualizes descriptions of anomalous, ESP-like receptive modes.
Mainstream synaesthesia is framed as a quirk of internal neural crosstalk inside the brain. In SORM framing, the SORM synaesthetic condition is not merely neural cross-wiring. It describes a mode of conscious coupling where the corthexus duty cycle shifts, opening awareness to multi-modal signals originating from the psychosheath and frequenbasins, rather than only ordinary local sensory input.

## SORM Definition
Within SORM, synaesthetic describes a responsive state of consciousness arising from a modified duty cycle of the observing locus. The duty cycle governs the ratio of time the corthexus locks to ordinary local sensory input versus sampling non-local resonant harmonics from the psychosheath and associated frequenbasins. When the duty cycle favours sampling across resonant domains rather than filtering strictly to standard sensory channels, percepts merge across normally separated modes of reception.
This is the mechanism behind apparent ESP or trans-sensory sensing in SORM: it is not telepathy as conventionally imagined, but synaesthetic resolution of information already present within shared frequenbasins. A frequenbasin does not carry only one kind of signal; its harmonics carry compound qualia — tone, texture, weight, colour, affective valence — all woven together. When the corthexus tuning relaxes its normal channel segregation, these bundled harmonics arrive in awareness fused together. The observer may “feel the texture of a frequency”, “hear the shape of a distant intent”, or “see the affective weight of an unspoken state”. These are not metaphor; they are direct synaesthetic readouts of the informational harmonics contained within a frequenbasin.
Crucially, this state is conditional. It depends on the current duty cycle: when the corthexus is operating in its default narrow-band mode, these compound signals are filtered out, partitioned, and suppressed to maintain stable ordinary perception of frozen moments. When duty cycle shifts to increase sampling bandwidth across the psychosheath, synaesthetic reception emerges. The condition is reversible, tunable, and tied directly to the coupling strength between corthexus and psychosheath. It is not a permanent gift or a supernatural power; it is a change in how the observer’s interface resolves the multi-modal information already resident in resonant domains.
