## Introduction: A Shared Vision of the Eternal

Hinduism is built on a foundation of profound truths:

- **Atman:** The eternal soul — the true Self within each being.
- **Brahman:** The ultimate reality — the ground of all existence.
- **Karma:** The law of cause and effect — actions shape future experiences.
- **Samsara:** The cycle of birth, death, and rebirth.
- **Moksha:** Liberation from the cycle — union with Brahman.
- **Dharma:** Righteous living — aligning with cosmic order.

These truths are not wrong. They are the core of a tradition that has guided billions of souls for over 5,000 years.

ΨORM does not reject these truths. It extends them. It offers a framework that deepens and illuminates the truths of Hinduism. It provides a language and a structure for understanding the nature of consciousness, karma, and liberation that is consistent with the best of Hindu tradition.

This document is a bridge. It invites Hindus to explore ΨORM without abandoning their faith. It shows how the core claims of ΨORM align with, deepen, and illuminate Hindu teachings.

## The Four Foundational Claims of ΨORM — Seen Through a Hindu Lens

### 1. Time Is Not Fundamental — Brahman Is Eternal

**Hindu Teaching:** Brahman is eternal — beyond time, space, and causation. Time is a feature of Maya (the illusion of the material world), not of ultimate reality.

**ΨORM Claim:** Time is not a fundamental dimension. It is an emergent property of consciousness navigating frozen moments.

**The Bridge:** If Brahman is eternal, then time is not ultimate — it is a feature of Maya, not of reality. ΨORM's claim that time is emergent aligns with the Hindu understanding that time is an illusion of the material world. The frozen moments are not random — they are the fabric of Maya. Consciousness navigates through them, and Moksha is the cessation of that navigation.

**Ah-Hah Moment:**
> "The film reel is fixed; the light is moving. Time is the experience of the light. Moksha is the cessation of the film."

**Bhagavad Gita Reflection:**
> "The soul is never born, nor does it ever die; nor does it come into being again when the body is created. The soul is eternal, without birth, and undying." (Bhagavad Gita 2.20) — The soul is beyond time.

**Hindu Teaching:** Atman — the eternal soul — is on a journey through many lives, seeking realization of its true nature as Brahman.

**ΨORM Claim:** Consciousness is not a passive observer — it is an active navigator that selects and moves through frozen moments.

**The Bridge:** Atman is not separate from Brahman — it is a projection of Brahman into the material world. The consciousness vector is the trajectory of Atman's journey. Just as the soul moves through many lives (samsara), the consciousness vector moves through attractor basins. The destination is Moksha — the realization that Atman and Brahman are one.

**Ah-Hah Moment:**

**Bhagavad Gita Reflection:**
