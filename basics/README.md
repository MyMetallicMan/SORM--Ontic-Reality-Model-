## The Two Realms: The Home and the Garden

For our consciousness, the configuration space consists of two primary experiences: **The Home** and **The Garden**.

### 1. The Home (The Non-Physical State)
Think of the Home as your permanent residence. It is where your soul lives. It is the non-physical state. It is the cloud. It is where you return between lives, where you rest, where you heal, and where you belong.

In the Home, you are not bound by a physical body. You are not filtered by a brain. You are your pure consciousness. You are surrounded by your soul group—the ones who have been with you across lifetimes. 

In the technical language of SORM, the Home is the *soul group tier* and the *frequenbasins* that resonate with your deepest identity.

### 2. The Garden (The Physical State)
Think of the Garden as a learning playground. It is where you go to grow, to learn, to play, and to experience things you cannot experience in the Home. 

The Garden is the physical universe. It is the Earth. It is the template you enter to learn your lessons. You are not here forever. You are here for a cycle. When the cycle is done, you return Home.

In the technical language of SORM, the Garden is the *ektoplax*—the external template administered by the *toponarch*.

---

## The Journey

Your consciousness is a traveler. 

You leave the Home. You enter the Garden. You put on a biological receiver (your body). You interact with other travelers. You experience joy, pain, love, and loss. You grow garbons. You learn the lessons you came to learn. 

And then, when the time comes, you leave the Garden. You return to the Home. You review your journey. You rest. You prepare. And eventually, you go back out into a new Garden to continue your growth.

This is the migration. This is the cycle. This is the adventure.

---

## Why Use Metaphors?

You might ask: *Why use metaphors like "Home" and "Garden" instead of just saying "non-physical state" and "physical universe"?*

Because metaphors are how the human brain learns. We understand the unknown by comparing it to the known. 

If I tell you, "You are an eternal consciousness navigating a high-dimensional configuration space," it sounds abstract and confusing. 
If I tell you, "You are a traveler who leaves your Home to go play and learn in a Garden," you understand it instantly.

The metaphors are not less true. They are simply more accessible.
