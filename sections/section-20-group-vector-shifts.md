## XX.6 The Hundredth Monkey Effect — A Case Study in Group Vector Coherence

### The Phenomenon

In the 1970s, Japanese macaques on Koshima Island were observed washing sweet potatoes in salt water — a behavior that spread through the troop. When a critical mass of monkeys (approximately 100) had adopted the behavior, monkeys on other islands — with no physical contact with the original troop — also began washing their potatoes.

This phenomenon, popularized as the "hundredth monkey effect," suggests a form of non-local information transfer that occurs once a critical threshold of group coherence is reached.
